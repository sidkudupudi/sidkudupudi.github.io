01

Problem & constraints

A planner benefits from knowing whether a pedestrian is about to cross, and a structured explanation makes that prediction auditable. Vision-language models can produce both, but they are slow and their accuracy has to be measured honestly against trivial baselines.

Does a small fine-tuned VLM actually learn crossing intent, and can it run beside a real-time tracker without hurting it?

02

My contribution

Dataset construction, fine-tuning, held-out evaluation and the asynchronous architecture

  • Built a JAAD clip-and-prompt dataset: 655 clips × 15 frames with context prompts and JSON targets.
  • Fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-2B with QLoRA (4-bit NF4, LoRA r = 16, α = 32, 5 epochs).
  • Evaluated base vs fine-tuned against a majority baseline on 131 held-out clips and traced the causes of the result.
  • Designed the asynchronous tracker + VLM architecture and logged telemetry over all 346 JAAD videos.

Failures, limits & next steps

✕It learned the format, not the decision

The fine-tuned model predicts 'Crossing' for 125 of 131 clips. Causes traced in the notebook: only padding is masked in the loss, so ~1,440 video tokens and the prompt are training targets and the answer is ~5% of the loss; 73% of training clips are 'Crossing'; each clip is 0.5 s seen as 4 frames; the confidence score in the targets is random and the reasoning templated.

Next steps · what would prove it

  • Mask the loss to the answer; rebalance or weight the intent token.
  • Longer clips (1.5–2 s), predict intent first, take confidence from the token probability.
  • Video-disjoint split.
  • Give the VLM its own stream priority or device to protect tracker latency.
