01

Problem & constraints

Wafer inspection and overlay metrology compare images that differ by fractions of a pixel. A naive cross-correlation peak is quantised to whole pixels, and zero-padding the spectrum to refine it is expensive.

02

My contribution

Implementation, verification and benchmarking

  • Implemented phase correlation and Guizar-Sicairos matrix-multiply DFT refinement with NumPy and SciPy only.
  • Cross-checked against scikit-image's reference implementation.
  • Generated four synthetic wafer topologies with a noise and optics model, using exact Fourier-domain shifts.

03

Method & key decisions

Integer peak, then a local DFT

The normalised cross-power spectrum keeps only phase; its inverse is a sharp peak at the shift. Instead of zero-padding by κ, the inverse DFT is evaluated only on a 1.5κ × 1.5κ grid around the coarse peak as two matrix products: O(κN²) instead of O(κ²N² log κN).

Test shifts without interpolation

Benchmark targets are shifted exactly in the frequency domain; bilinear or bicubic warping would blur the image and bias the result.

Porting it exposed a weak spot

The browser port gives the same estimate as the NumPy library on every logic and DRAM case, but differs by up to 0.09 px on reticle and power patterns. The cause: those clean patterns have exact spectral nulls, where the cross-power spectrum is about 1e-14 and its phase is FFT rounding noise. Phase normalisation amplifies those bins to full weight. Dropping them in a follow-up experiment cut the error on every pattern, most on power/MEMS (2.3×).

Failures, limits & next steps

Limitations

  • Best-case benchmark: pure circular translation, the model the method assumes.
  • The 0.01 px grid is a floor at κ = 100.

Next steps · what would prove it

  • Fold null-bin masking or magnitude weighting into the library and re-run the benchmark.
  • Hann apodisation before the FFT; a log-polar stage for rotation and scale.
  • Validation on real optical overlay images; a batched CUDA port.
