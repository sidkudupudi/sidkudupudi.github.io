Industrial defect segmentation with a C++ TensorRT app

I trained YOLO11-seg on MVTec AD to 0.82 mask mAP50, wrote the C++ TensorRT app that serves it, and found that rebalancing VisA's data lifted mask mAP50 from 0.25 to 0.41 where a bigger model did nothing.

01

Problem & constraints

Visual inspection needs a mask, not just a box: the defect's extent decides whether a part is scrap. The model must work on parts it has never seen and must not reject good ones.

Does a pixel-level detector trained on a few hundred defect images hold up on held-out parts, and what actually moves its accuracy?

02

My contribution

Dataset engineering, training experiments, evaluation and the C++ inference application

  • Converted MVTec AD masks to YOLO polygon labels with an 80/20 split per defect type.
  • Ran the VisA rebalancing experiment and the 2.2×-larger-model comparison.
  • Measured false alarms on 467 defect-free parts the model never saw in training.
  • Wrote the C++ app: pinned CHW buffers, async H2D/enqueueV3/D2H on one stream, NMS and mask reconstruction from prototypes.
  • Scored the app's masks case by case against ground truth.

Method & key decisions

Data balance over model size

The two VisA runs have identical settings; only the share of background images changed (kept at 10% of the defect count). That removed a 20-epoch stall at zero and raised mask mAP50 by 16 points. A 2.2× larger model at 4× the pixels gave no gain on MVTec.

Mask decoding in C++

The app decodes 21,504 proposals × (4 box + 15 class + 32 mask coefficients). Each instance mask is sigmoid(coefficients · prototypes), upsampled and cropped to its box.

Fail loudly on stale engines

A TensorRT engine only loads with the version that built it. After an upgrade the app crashed on the old engine, so it now checks the load and says why.

07

Failures, limits & next steps

✕Right class, wrong extent

On the transistor's bent lead the mask spreads over the whole component (IoU 0.10). Shape and position defects are harder to delineate than surface marks.

✕Missed: a 0.1% defect

The damaged screw thread covers about 1,200 pixels, 0.1% of the image, and nothing clears the app's 0.5 confidence threshold.

✕False alarm on a good part

A small surface speck on a defect-free hazelnut is flagged; the other defect-free part, a screw, stays clean.

Limitations

  • No defect-free images in training; image-level AUROC and pixel-level PRO (the MVTec benchmark metrics) are not reported yet.
  • Hyperparameter evolution finished 2 of 20 iterations on a 16 GB GPU.
  • The engine is built from an FP32 graph; C++ end-to-end timing including pre/post-processing is not yet measured.

Next steps · what would prove it

  • Train with defect-free backgrounds and report AUROC / PRO.
  • FP16 export and end-to-end C++ timing.
  • Smaller proxy model for hyperparameter search.
