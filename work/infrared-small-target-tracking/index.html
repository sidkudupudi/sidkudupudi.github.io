01

Problem & constraints

Infrared search-and-track systems watch the sky for aircraft and drones. At long range a target covers 3–7 pixels of a 1280×1024 frame and is only 10–16 grey levels brighter than cloud, haze and ground clutter.

A detector that misses it loses the track; one that fires on clutter floods the operator with false alarms. Either way the pipeline has to keep up with the camera.

Can a 3–7 pixel target be detected and tracked reliably in cluttered infrared video, fast enough to run on every frame?

Constraints

  • Target smaller than the stride of a standard detector's finest feature map (8 px).
  • Full-frame processing, no tiling, within a 33 ms budget at 30 fps.
  • The production port must reproduce the prototype's outputs, not just run faster.

02

My contribution

Design, implementation and evaluation: enhancement, detector, tracker, C++/CUDA port and validation

  • Designed the enhancement stage (dark-channel dehaze + white top-hat) and measured its signal-to-clutter gain.
  • Added a stride-4 (P2) head to YOLOv8n and chose the operating point on held-out full frames.
  • Built and tuned a constant-acceleration Kalman tracker with χ² gating and Hungarian assignment.
  • Ported every stage to C++17/CUDA/TensorRT with pybind11 bindings, then validated it stage by stage against the Python reference.

04

Method & key decisions

Enhancement before learning

The white top-hat keeps only bright features smaller than the structuring element, so clouds and buildings largely vanish. The raw, dehazed and top-hat maps are stacked as the detector's three input channels; mean signal-to-clutter ratio rises from 2.48 (raw) to 8.72 (top-hat).

A detection head at the target's scale

YOLOv8n's finest default head predicts on 8-pixel cells, already larger than the target. A stride-4 P2 head gives 4-pixel cells; the network stays fully convolutional so it runs on the whole frame.

Operating point chosen on held-out frames

At confidence 0.3 the detector finds 97.9% of targets with 0.047 false alarms per frame. The tracker can reject an isolated false alarm but cannot recover a target the detector never reports, so recall was favoured.

Noise model from the sensor, not from tuning

With measurement noise r = 1 px the constant-velocity filter produced up to 35 ID switches per sequence, because the detector's centre jitters by about 2 px. Setting r = 2 px, measured from the detector, gave one track per target. Constant acceleration then cut track error from 1.98 to 1.27 px, but its 30-frame forecast error was 5× larger (56 vs 11 px), so look-ahead prediction should use constant velocity.

Failures, limits & next steps

Limitations

  • Evaluated on single-target sequences; crossings between targets are untested.
  • Detection is scored with a 6 px centre-distance rule rather than mask IoU.
  • Benchmark excludes image decoding; end-to-end latency from a live sensor is not yet measured.

Next steps · what would prove it

  • Multi-target sequences with crossings, reporting ID switches and IDF1.
  • Sensor-to-track latency from a live camera stream.
  • Profile the same pipeline on a Jetson-class device.
